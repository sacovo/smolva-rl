Critic-Guided Rollout Post-Training of a Compact Vision-Language-Action Policy for Edge Robotics

Sandro Covo · FHNW, Switzerland

Paper (PDF) Code & data BibTeX

Same task, same model size. The difference is post-training: rollouts scored by a learned critic, labeled +/−, and fed back — the labeling scheme this page borrows for every video on it.

TL;DR

RECAP-style critic-guided rollout post-training — introduced with the 4.9B-parameter π*0.6 — transfers to SmolVLA, a 450M flow-matching VLA small enough for battery-powered robots. Unlike RECAP, rollouts are collected fully autonomously and labeled by the learned critic alone, with no human corrections. Fine-tuning on critic-labeled rollouts mixed with expert demonstrations reaches 67.8% average success on three LIBERO suites, vs. 62.9% for the base policy and 52.4% for plain SmolVLA. One-step SnapFlow distillation then cuts action-chunk latency 3.6× on a Jetson Orin Nano.

Findings

What we learned

  • F1The knowledge-insulation recipe is the largest single effect. Pre-training with KI, FAST co-training, and advantage conditioning gains ≈10 points per suite over the plain SmolVLA recipe (+10.2, +9.8, +11.4).
  • F2Rollout-only fine-tuning is not uniformly safe. It lifts the short-horizon suites (+6.6 spatial, +4.2 goal) but degrades long-horizon LIBERO-10 (35.8 vs. 41.6) — a compact policy seems to need explicit replay to retain behavior.
  • F3Co-training with expert demonstrations fixes the regression. Mixing demonstration batches (ratio 0.5) into the fine-tune lifts all three suites and gives the best policy: 67.8% average. It matters most for skills absent from the policy's own rollouts — rollout data can only sharpen what the policy already does.
  • F4The advantage-labeling threshold must be permissive. Aggressive thresholds (f⁺ = 0.3–0.4) label necessary approach and transport motions negative; the policy partially unlearns them and guidance amplifies the damage. f⁺ = 0.8 avoids this.
  • F5The gains come from training, not inference-time guidance. Classifier-free guidance adds only ~1 point at its best setting; nearly the whole improvement is already present at w = 0.
  • F6One-step distillation makes it real-time on edge hardware. SnapFlow replaces the ten-step flow-matching solve with a single step: 922 → 255 ms per 20-action chunk on a Jetson Orin Nano (8 GB).

Failure modes

Where every policy still fails

The dominant remaining failure is long-horizon tasks chaining two pick-and-place subgoals: every method stays below ~40% on that subset, and below 16% on the hardest task. The policy typically places the first object and runs out of time sequencing the second.

Edge latency

SnapFlow one-step inference on Jetson Orin Nano

The target hardware is a 7–25 W board with 8 GB of unified memory — too small for the bfloat16 weights of π*0.6, comfortable for SmolVLA. Distilling the ten-step flow-matching solve into a single step raises the re-planning rate from 1.1 to 3.9 action chunks per second.
